## Domains where women often show an average advantage

### Education / credentials
- NCES Digest table (US degrees by sex over time): https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d23/tables/dt23_318.10.asp?current=yes  
- NCES COE — women majority of associate’s (~63%) and bachelor’s (~59%) in 2021–22: https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/cta  
- AIR summary of NCES forecast (women predominate; lead projected to hold/grow at bachelor’s/master’s): https://www.air.org/resource/blog-post/nces-forecast-degrees-increase-and-women-predominate  
- IZA review — stylized fact: females higher educational attainment/achievement in many settings: https://docs.iza.org/dp14074.pdf  
- UK gov note — women on average more highly educated in their GPG analysis framing: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/understanding-the-gender-pay-gap-in-the-uk/understanding-the-gender-pay-gap-in-the-uk-drivers-and-barriers-evidence-from-uklhs-2009-to-2023

### Verbal / episodic memory (usually small average effects)
- Meta-analysis (PMC) — female advantage phonemic fluency, recall, recognition; small ds; some publication-bias notes: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9896545/  
- PLOS ONE — female advantage verbal episodic memory across many countries: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0214945  
- HUNT / verbal learning discussion (female advantage revisited): https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658211.2023.2203431  
- PNAS — cognitive gender differences; women often stronger episodic memory / reading literacy in reported patterns: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319538111

### Longevity
- Our World in Data — women live longer than men (global gap; country charts): https://ourworldindata.org/why-do-women-live-longer-than-men  
- Max Planck Institute explainer: https://www.age.mpg.de/why-do-women-live-longer-than-men  
- PMC discussion of gendered life expectancy: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7829786/

## Honest counterweight (same literature family — do not ignore)

Many of the same overviews also report **male average advantages** in domains such as visuospatial / some math patterns, and note **gender similarities** on a large share of cognitive tasks (Hyde’s gender-similarities theme is widely cited in this literature). Education papers also note field-of-study segregation (e.g. men overrepresented in some higher-paid STEM paths). Longevity advantage coexists with women often reporting more illness/disability in later life (“morbidity–mortality paradox” discussions).

So: real female advantages exist in **several important domains**. They do **not** add up to a clean “superior in all aspects” spreadsheet. You asked to see the articles anyway — here they are.
