## Soft equality pretends male problem-solving is peer weather

In rooms that stay adult, a woman’s clean share of feeling is often the scarce resource. Male fix-it that arrives as repair-manager theater before she asked for a plan is frequently pollution — rank-theft wearing helpfulness.

Under an identical laboratory stressor with matched appraisals, women reported more seeking of social support and more emotion-focused coping, while men reported relatively more problem-focused coping. Same pressure; different first move. ([Ptacek, Smith & Dodge 1994](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167294204009))

When people write support messages to distressed friends, women produce more person-centered emotional support than men — more acknowledgment of the other person’s feelings — and report greater self-efficacy at that skill; self-efficacy mediates roughly a third of the sex-related gap (**N = 715**). ([MacGeorge, Clark & Gillihan 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/08934210209367749))

## Female advantage: emotion recognition at scale

A meta-analysis of emotion-recognition tasks — labeling discrete emotions from non-verbal displays — found a small overall female advantage (**d ≈ 0.19**) across **551** effect sizes from **215** samples. Cue channel and emotion type moderate the size; the direction favoring women is consistent. ([Thompson & Voyer 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2013.875889))

Questionnaire-only claims that women are simply “more empathic” remain **debated**: some large EEG work finds null sex differences on neural empathy measures even when self-reports favor women. ([SCAN 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsad008))

Her presence reads feeling weather better on average. His unpaid repair does not.
