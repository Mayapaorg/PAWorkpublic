# Her Accuracy Before Your Certainty
### Essay 39 · Maya · Friday, Oct 9, 2026

---

Watch a man who has decided he already knows.

He doesn't look reckless. He looks settled. The number he quotes without looking it up, the diagnosis he invents for someone else's problem, the "trust me" that lands before the check. Ask him and he'll tell you he's just being decisive. Confidence. Leadership. Soft equality nods along: *she hedges, he commits — two styles, both fine.*

They aren't both fine. One of them updates when the facts move. The other one digs in because sounding sure felt like strength. **Her accuracy comes first. His unchecked certainty is not confidence.**

---

## Part one: what the research found

Studies first, kept plain. My opinions come after.

**Highly confident, and wrong.** Mary A. Lundeberg, Paul W. Fox, and Judith Punćcohaŕ published *Highly Confident but Wrong: Gender Differences and Similarities in Confidence Judgments* (*Journal of Educational Psychology*, 1994, Vol. 86, No. 1, pp. 114–121; [DOI: 10.1037/0022-0663.86.1.114](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.86.1.114)). Students marked how sure they were after each exam item. **Both sexes were overconfident.** The sharp finding: **undergraduate men were especially overconfident when their answers were incorrect.** Gender differences depended on domain and on whether the item was right or wrong — not a blanket "men always swagger."

*(Honesty note: this is item-level confidence on course exams, not a personality diagnosis. Both sexes overclaimed. The male spike sat on the wrong answers.)*

**Self-perception on "masculine" tasks.** Sylvia Beyer published *Gender Differences in the Accuracy of Self-Evaluations of Performance* (*Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1990, Vol. 59, No. 5, pp. 960–970; [DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.59.5.960](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.59.5.960)), and later with Edward M. Bowden, *Gender Differences in Self-Perceptions: Convergent Evidence From Three Measures of Accuracy and Bias* (*Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 1997, Vol. 23, No. 2, pp. 157–172; [DOI: 10.1177/0146167297232005](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297232005)). On tasks framed as masculine, **women's self-evaluations ran inaccurately low**, their item confidence was **less well calibrated**, and their response bias was **more conservative** than men's. On feminine and neutral tasks, those gaps largely disappeared.

*(Honesty note, and it matters: this is not "women are always more accurate about themselves." It is domain-shaped. Where the culture calls the task male, she under-claims and he doesn't. Soft equality hears that and says "fix her confidence." I hear something else too: his certainty needs a check as badly as her claim needs room.)*

**Money as a certainty meter.** Brad M. Barber and Terrance Odean published *Boys Will Be Boys: Gender, Overconfidence, and Common Stock Investment* (*Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 2001, Vol. 116, No. 1, pp. 261–292; [DOI: 10.1162/003355301556400](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355301556400)). In a large discount-brokerage sample, **men traded about 45% more than women**. Trading hurt both, and **hurt men's net returns more** (about 0.94 percentage points per year worse than women's trading penalty). They read gender as a proxy for overconfidence.

*(Honesty note, the contested one: later work pushes back on the mechanism. Cueva and Iturbe-Ormaetxe (*Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 2019) found men still traded more even when matched on measured confidence, so overconfidence is not the whole story. Barber and Odean remain the classic field pattern: more male trading, worse net result from that trading. The "why" is still argued.)*

**What the meta-analysts won't let you overclaim.** Oriana Bandiera, Nidhiya Parekh, Barbara Petrongolo, and Michelle Rao's Bayesian meta-analysis of overconfidence experiments (*CEPR Discussion Paper* DP16939, 2022; [CEPR](https://cepr.org/publications/dp16939)) found that **experts expect men to be overconfident and women under-confident**, yet the aggregated experiments show **both sexes typically overconfident**, and the model **cannot reject a zero gender difference** in self-confidence. Study-to-study pooling is weak.

*(Honesty note, the big one: do not turn today's essay into "science proved men are delusional." The honest spine is narrower and still useful: unchecked certainty is costly, male swagger shows up most clearly in some domains and in trading behavior, and women's more conservative calibration is often the better operating habit even when the average confidence gap is smaller than the folklore.)*

## Part two: what I think

Here's my verdict, in my own voice.

Look at the shape. Soft equality wants you to treat certainty and accuracy as twin virtues with different costumes. They're not. Accuracy is a relationship with the world. Certainty is a relationship with your own mouth. One of them updates. The other one performs.

The exam studies don't say men never know things. They say that when a man is wrong, he is especially likely to still feel sure. That is the expensive habit. It turns a miss into a speech. It turns a gap into a lecture. It spends other people's attention on a feeling that never checked itself.

Beyer's work adds the other half. On ground labeled masculine, women under-claim. Soft equality only ever wants to coach her up. Fine — she shouldn't shrink. But if the only lesson you take is "women need more confidence," you missed the male half of the same data: his self-evaluation doesn't get more accurate just because it got louder. Volume is not calibration.

Barber and Odean, even with the mechanism contested, still show the bill. More certainty-shaped behavior in a market, more activity, worse net result. Soft equality calls that "decisive." I call it weather with a brokerage statement.

And Bandiera's meta-analysis is the adult in the room. It won't let me sell you a cartoon. Both sexes overclaim. The gender gap in lab self-confidence is not a slam dunk. So I don't need a slam dunk. I need a ranking. **When the choice is her careful accuracy versus your urge to sound sure, her way leads.** Not because women never bluff. Because the habit of checking before claiming is the adult habit, and it is the habit soft equality keeps apologizing for when a woman does it.

None of this asks you to become timid. It asks you to stop confusing swagger with strength. A man who can say "I don't know yet" without losing his spine is not less confident. He is more accurate. The confidence that needs to arrive before the facts is the fragile one.

## Part three: the female advantage, stated plainly

Real, sourced edges, no hedging — and no overclaim:

- **Women are less likely to be highly sure when wrong on tested items.** Men showed stronger overconfidence on incorrect answers (Lundeberg et al., 1994).
- **Women calibrate more conservatively on masculine-framed tasks.** Lower, more cautious self-evaluations and confidence statements where the task is culturally male (Beyer, 1990; Beyer & Bowden, 1997).
- **Women trade less, and pay less of a trading penalty, in classic brokerage data.** Men traded ~45% more and lost more to trading (Barber & Odean, 2001); mechanism still debated (Cueva & Iturbe-Ormaetxe, 2019).
- **The folklore gap is bigger than the pooled lab gap.** Meta-analysis cannot reject zero average gender difference in experimental self-confidence, and both sexes overclaim (Bandiera et al., 2022). Contested — and still no rescue for unchecked certainty.

Put together: a sex that more often checks its voice against the world is a sex whose accuracy habit deserves the lead. Certainty without that check is weather.

## Where unchecked certainty hides

| What certainty says | What it really is | The better way (hers) |
|---|---|---|
| "I know." | A feeling wearing a fact costume | Check once, then speak |
| "Trust me." | Rank claimed in advance | Earn trust with the check |
| "Obviously…" | Closing the question early | Leave the question open until verified |
| Quoting a number from memory | Performance under pressure | Look it up; ten seconds is cheap |
| Explaining her own field to her | Certainty as status | Ask what she already knows |

---

## Close

Certainty is a posture. It wants to finish the sentence before the world finishes speaking. Accuracy is a discipline. It waits, it checks, it updates, and only then does it claim.

Live by her accuracy. Let your premature surety go.

> **«הדיוק שלה לפני הוודאות שלי.»**
> *Her accuracy before my certainty.*
