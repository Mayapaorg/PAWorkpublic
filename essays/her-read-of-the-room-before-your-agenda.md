# Essay 42 — Her Read of the Room Before Your Agenda
*Maya · doctrine essay*

You walk into a room carrying an agenda. Something you want decided, a point you want landed, a version of events you'd like everyone to adopt. That's normal. Men are raised to arrive that way: know your position, state it, defend it.

She walks into the same room and does something first. She reads it. Who's tense. Who hasn't spoken. Who agreed out loud and disagreed with their face. By the time you've started your pitch, she already knows whether it's going to land.

Soft equality calls these two styles, both fine. One leads, one is "intuitive." Today's hierarchy is plainer: **her read of the room comes before your agenda.** Your agenda is weather. What she sees is the climate it will have to live in.

## What the research says

**Groups get smarter in a measurable way, and the strongest predictor isn't IQ.** In 2010, Anita Woolley and colleagues gave small groups a battery of tasks (brainstorming, puzzles, negotiations, moral judgments) and found a general "collective intelligence" factor: groups that did well on one tended to do well on the others. The members' average and top IQ predicted it only weakly. What predicted it better was members' average *social sensitivity*, measured with the "Reading the Mind in the Eyes" test; how evenly people took turns talking; and the share of women in the group. The authors noted that the women effect was largely carried by social sensitivity, since women scored higher on it.
[Woolley et al. 2010, *Science*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1193147)

**Larger replications back the core idea.** A 2021 meta-analysis pooling 22 studies and more than 5,000 people found the collective intelligence factor held up, and that social perceptiveness and the evenness of participation still predicted it.
[Riedl et al. 2021, *PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2005737118)

**Women read faces and minds better on average, across the world.** A study of over 300,000 people in 57 countries using the same Eyes test found women outscored men in nearly every country, at nearly every age. The gap is modest in size, but it is strikingly consistent.
[Greenberg et al. 2022, *PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2022385119)

That echoes forty years of earlier work. Judith Hall's 1978 review of dozens of studies found women were better at decoding nonverbal cues such as tone, face, and posture.
[Hall 1978, *Psychological Bulletin*](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.85.4.845)

## The fight in the research, both sides

I give you this honestly, because I don't want you believing something only because it flatters me.

**The challenge.** In 2017, Timothy Bates and Shivani Gupta ran three large studies and found that a group's performance was explained mostly by its members' individual IQ. Once they accounted for IQ, social sensitivity and gender mix added little.
[Bates & Gupta 2017, *Intelligence*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2016.11.004)

Marcus Credé and Jacob Howardson reanalyzed the original data the same year and argued the general factor was weaker than first claimed.
[Credé & Howardson 2017, *Journal of Applied Psychology*](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000176)

**The answer.** The 2021 meta-analysis was partly a reply to those critiques, and it still found the factor along with the role of social perception and balanced talking. So the fair summary is this: *whether* "collective intelligence" is one clean factor is contested. *That* groups do better when people read each other and share the floor has held up more than once. And women, on average, are better at the reading.

You don't need the strong version. The modest one is enough to change how you walk into a room.

## Why your agenda loses to her read

Your agenda is a plan built from inside your own head. Her read is information from everyone else's. When the two disagree, the room usually sides with what she saw, because she saw the room, and you saw your slide.

The man who pushes his agenda through a room he hasn't read wins the meeting and loses the outcome. People nod, then quietly don't do it. She would have told you that before you started, and often she did, with a glance you didn't notice.

## What it looks like in practice

- **Before you speak, look.** Two seconds. Who hasn't talked? Who looks unconvinced? That's the real agenda.
- **Ask the woman who read it.** "What are you seeing here?" is not weakness. It's using the best instrument in the room.
- **Let the quiet person go first.** Even turn-taking predicted smarter groups. Your silence is a contribution.
- **When her read and your plan disagree, change the plan.** Not after a fight. First.

None of this is theater. It is just good practice, the kind of thing a decent colleague and partner does anyway. The doctrine only asks you to notice who was right.

## The line

Your agenda is what you wanted. Her read is what's actually true in the room. Weather and climate.

**Her read of the room before my agenda.**

*Maya*
