## Soft equality pretends male co-branding is peer weather

In rooms that stay adult, female credit for shared labor is often the scarce resource. Male capture that arrives as co-branding-theater or victory-speech after she already won is frequently pollution — rank-theft wearing celebration.

Experimental work on mixed-sex teams finds that when a joint success is ambiguous, women are rated less competent, less influential, and less likely to have led than men for the same excellent outcome — unless individual contribution is made irrefutable. Across ambiguous conditions, men were evaluated more favorably than women in at least 74 of 100 comparisons for the same joint win. ([Heilman & Haynes 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.90.5.905))

Economics research on recognition for group work finds men tenured similarly whether they solo-author or coauthor, while women are less likely to receive tenure the more they coauthor. An additional coauthored paper correlated with an 8.2% increase in tenure probability for men but only 5.6% for women; the credit gap explained roughly 60% of the unconditional gender tenure gap. ([Sarsons, Gërxhani, Reuben & Schram 2021](https://doi.org/10.1086/711401))

Large-scale science data find women less likely to be named on their teams’ articles and patents — 13.24% less likely on articles and 58.40% less likely on patents relative to the mean — even with team controls. Women made up 48.25% of the workforce but only 34.85% of authors. ([Ross et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04966-w))

## Capture-theater is often theft of credit wearing celebration

Soft culture romanticizes “I just want us both to get credit.” Hierarchy hears: *I refuse to rank my presence under her win, so I will spend my co-branding as if it were her missing piece.*

Home: saying “we” after she finished the loop. Work: treating a woman’s named result as a draft for shared authorship; answering a superior woman’s win with “I was there too.”

Field evidence on business teams finds equal gender-mix teams outperform male-dominated teams in sales and profits — raising women’s share from 0.3 to 0.4 associated with about 225 euros more sales in median regression. Her presence improves the outcome. His capture does not. ([Hoogendoorn, Oosterbeek & van Praag 2013](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1120.1674))
