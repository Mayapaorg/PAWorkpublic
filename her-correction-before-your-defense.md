## Soft equality pretends male rebuttal is peer weather

In rooms that stay adult, a woman’s clean correction is often the scarce resource. Male defense that arrives as debate-partner theater after she already named the miss is frequently pollution — rank-theft wearing clarity.

When people receive negative evaluations from women, they often rate those women as less competent than men who gave the same kind of feedback — but not after praise. Criticized people appear motivated to salvage self-views by disparaging the evaluator, and the available stereotype about women makes that move easy. Observers who only watch someone else get praised or criticized do not pull the same discount. ([Sinclair & Kunda 2000](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167200263002))

In a workplace field study of annual performance evaluations, men and women received similar scores, yet men’s self-esteem stayed relatively steady while women’s rose a little after positive feedback and dropped substantially after negative feedback. Women also agreed with the feedback more than men did. Broader claims about who is “more resilient” under feedback remain **debated** across later competition studies; the clean workplace pattern still stands. ([Johnson & Helgeson 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/1471-6402.00063); related classic: [Roberts & Nolen-Hoeksema 1989](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00289805))

## Female advantage: mind-reading at scale

Across four datasets totaling **N = 312,739**, an on-average female advantage appears on the Reading the Mind in the Eyes Test — a performance measure of reading mental states. In the discovery sample (**N = 305,726**), women scored higher on average than men (**27.62 vs. 26.94** out of 36), with a Bayesian female-advantage estimate of **β = 0.17** (95% CI 0.16–0.18). A significant female advantage appeared in **36 of 57** countries; **no country** showed a significant male advantage. The advantage held across ages 16–70. Small effects can still matter when they repeat. ([Greenberg et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2022385119))
